1. Camera Setup

Grant camera access, then place your object on a plain, non-reflective turntable or lazy-susan in even, diffuse lighting (avoid direct sun/hard shadows — they confuse feature matching). Leave ~1m clearance around the object so the camera can stay at a fixed distance while it rotates.

2. Background Plate

Remove the object from the frame (keep the camera and turntable exactly where they'll be during capture) and take one clean shot of the empty background. The backend uses this to automatically mask the object out from the background in every photo, which substantially improves reconstruction quality. It also unlocks a live outline overlay on the video below, right after you place the object back in frame, so you can see exactly what the app will treat as "the object" before you take any photos or start recording.

3. Turntable Capture

Place the object back in frame. After each shot, rotate it a small, even amount (~10°) and capture again, working all the way around a full 360° turn. Aim for 36 shots minimum; more is better for fine detail.

Important: don't keep the camera at one fixed height/angle the whole way around — a pure flat turntable spin is a classic weak case for 3D reconstruction and tends to produce a flattened, "2D-looking" result. Instead, vary the camera's height or tilt as you go: capture roughly a third of your shots angled slightly down from above, a third level with the object, and a third angled slightly up from below. That real viewpoint variation is what gives the reconstruction actual depth to work with.
